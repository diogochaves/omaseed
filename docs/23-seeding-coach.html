The seeding coach

qBittorrent can already do most of what keeps a library seeding: categories that file torrents into folders, share limits, queue rules, an alt-speed schedule, an incomplete folder. Most people never touch those settings because nothing tells them what each one is for. omaseed knows the real library and the real server settings, so it can say what a setting would change for you: which torrents, how many bytes, which peers. This board proposes a coach that only speaks when it has found something, ranks what it says by what it costs your seeding, and fixes it with one key.

Every torrent name, path, count and size in the mocks is example data. Switch the theme in the bar below: the mocks use Theme roles only.

3 · the apps around it

Radarr, Sonarr, cross-seed: where seeding usually dies

How the *arr apps use qBittorrent

  • Radarr and Sonarr (with Prowlarr for indexers) add a torrent under their own category (radarr, tv-sonarr) and watch it.
  • When it finishes, "completed download handling" imports it into the media library. With hardlinks the import is instant and the torrent keeps its files. Without them it is a copy, or a move, and a move leaves qBittorrent with missingFiles.
  • Hardlinks only work inside one filesystem, so the usual layout is one root, /data/torrents beside /data/media. The TRaSH guides teach exactly this.
  • A torrent the *arr imported by moving it stops seeding without telling anyone. That is the most common way a seeding library quietly shrinks.

Cross-seed

  • The same files are often listed on several trackers. Cross-seeding adds the other trackers' torrents pointed at the files you already have, so one copy on disk seeds in every swarm.
  • qui does it by asking indexers (Torznab feeds from Prowlarr or Jackett) for matches, then adding them with the right category and save path. It reuses the files, or builds a hardlink or reflink tree when names differ.
  • cross-seed (the standalone tool) and autobrr do the same from RSS and announces.
  • All of them need indexer access and API keys for the trackers. That is server-side automation, which the charter keeps out of omaseed.

What omaseed can see from where it sits

  • Categories named like an *arr's, and torrents in them that went missingFiles right after finishing: the move happened.
  • Two torrents with the same content_path: already cross-seeded. Board 22 already counts their files once.
  • Private-tracker torrents (private) whose seed time is short of a typical minimum. Trackers don't publish their rules through qBittorrent, so the minimum has to come from the user.

4 · the idea

A coach that only speaks when it has found something

Specific. Every finding names your torrents, counts and bytes: "34 complete torrents are queued and seed to nobody; you are the only seed of 3". A generic tip ("did you know queueing exists?") is never shown.
Ranked by what it costs your seeding. Three levels: stops seeding (port closed, files moved away, a limit stopped a rare torrent), slows seeding (queue caps, slots, limits), tidiness (no categories, no incomplete folder).
One key to fix, and you see it first. enter opens the exact setting or sheet, already filled in, and you confirm there. Nothing changes on the server behind your back.
Quiet by default. One "did you know" a day at most, each shown once, only when a real situation triggers it. Anything can be dismissed for good. No tips carousel, no badges.

7 · surface three

Categories with folders, set up once

One sheet turns "41 torrents in Downloads" into a layout. It proposes a folder per category from what the library holds, shows what would move and whether that is instant (same disk) or a copy, and turns on the "relocate when the category changes" switch. Nothing moves until you press the button.

File torrents by category

Each category gets its own folder under one root. Setting a torrent's category then moves its files there; qBittorrent does the move, with no recheck.

8 · surface four

Living next to Radarr and Sonarr

omaseed stays a viewer and a friend of the *arr apps. It never automates them. What it can do:

  • Recognise their categories (radarr, tv-sonarr, lidarr, or ones you mark) and draw a small mark on them in the rail, so you know not to rename or move those torrents by hand.
  • Notice the move: torrents that go missingFiles soon after finishing, in one of those categories. That becomes the check-up finding and the N4 moment.
  • Explain hardlinks once, in your paths: "your torrents are in /data/torrents, your library is on another disk, so Radarr can only copy or move". It links to the TRaSH guide for the setup.
  • Guard their torrents: the delete sheet warns that the *arr may grab the release again, and a rename asks first.

Cross-seed, honestly

Finding cross-seeds needs indexer access (Prowlarr or Jackett feeds) and tracker API keys. qui and cross-seed already do that well, on the server. omaseed should not grow an indexer client. It can still:

  • show torrents that share files as one item: "seeded on 3 trackers, one copy on disk" (board 22 already counts the files once);
  • say, when it sees a qui instance or a cross-seed tag, where cross-seeding happens, and link there;
  • later, if wanted, read qui's API to show its cross-seed results in the console (D8 b).
9 · surface five

Seeding goals instead of limits, and encouragement without confetti

qBittorrent calls them share limits and frames them as a stop. Most people set one to stop seeding, then forget the torrents it stops. omaseed can show the same numbers as a goal: "seed to 2.0 or 14 days, and keep going if I'm one of the last seeds". The goal is qBittorrent's limit underneath. The "keep if rare" part is the rare guard: omaseed sets a rare torrent's own limit to "none", and says so (D7).

  • Private trackers: hit-and-run rules (for example 7 days of seed time) and bonus points for rare torrents are each tracker's own, and none of it reaches qBittorrent. A tracker row can take a minimum seed time from the user (D7). The delete sheet and the goals then honour it.
10 · lines not to cross

What the coach is not

Not an engine or an automation layer. No RSS rules, no indexers, no cross-seed search, no *arr control (charter §4 "never").
Never moves files on its own. Every move is qBittorrent's, after a button you pressed in a sheet that showed the move first.
Not a tips feed. No rotating hints, no onboarding tour, nothing on a timer. A finding exists because the library shows it.
Not gamified. No points, badges or levels. Numbers that mean something, said plainly.
Not nagging. Dismiss once and it stays dismissed, for that finding on that server.
11 · building it

How it would be built

core

  • Coach: pure rules over the library, the categories, app/preferences (ServerPreferences already loads them), server_state and board 22's ledger. Each rule returns findings {id, level, title, detail, fix}, with one QtTest per rule, on fixtures.
  • Dismissals and "shown once" go in state.json per server, or in clientdata (D10).
  • A rule runs when its data changes, never on a timer of its own.

views

  • The check-up pane in SettingsModal, before Speed. Each fix is an existing pane or sheet with a field preset (docs/16's rows already write each key).
  • The add sheet's folder line (N1), the limit toast (N2) on board 22's T7 spot, the delete sheet line (N3), the moment line (N4).
  • The categories sheet (§7) and the goals sheet (§9), each a Kit Sheet.

checks

  • A box qBittorrent (app-box.sh up --qbt) set up badly on purpose: queue at 2, a 1.0 ratio limit, UPnP off, no categories. A new coach-walk.sh checks every finding, every fix and every dismissal on that server and on disk.
  • An "*arr" case: a fixture category radarr whose files a script moves away after completion.
Like board 22, this departs from the frozen spec: once ruled, a REVIEW-v0.1.0.md line and one issue per surface. The check-up's findings feed board 22's attention line, so the two boards share the order of work (D9).
