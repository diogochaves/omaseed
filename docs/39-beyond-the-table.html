Beyond the table

omaseed today is a very good torrent client that looks like every other torrent client, dressed in Omarchy. Transmission once felt like a revolution because each row said what was happening in plain words. Nobody has moved since. This board goes looking for the next step: new ways to see the library, the details of one torrent, and adding one, while keeping everything that makes omaseed feel like Omarchy (theme roles, the monospace face, square corners, the keyboard).

§1 says why every client looks the same. §2 is what other apps do better. §3 sets five questions a client should answer before it shows any mechanism. §4 is a live window: switch layouts with "display" (or v once you've clicked into it), click any torrent to open its story, press "+" to add one. §5–§11 take each idea apart with what it costs. §12 is how it would reach the app (opt-in or not), §13 what the API already gives, and §14 your calls. The theme switch repaints every mock.

1 · the starting point

Why every torrent client looks the same

the table shows the engineTen columns of mechanism, all equal. Seeds, peers, swarm, ratio, ETA sit side by side at the same weight. The one thing you came to know ("is it ready?") is one column among ten.
state, not meaning"stalled", "queued", "stopped" are engine words. They say what the engine is doing, not what it means for you or what you could do. "Stalled" could be fine (a quiet seed) or fatal (no seed exists).
no sense of timeEverything is a snapshot. Nothing shows when things will land, what landed while you were away, or how a torrent got here. Time is the axis people think in.
names are for machines"Cosmos.Laundromat.2015.2160p.WEB-DL.CC-BY". Release names carry a title, a year, a resolution and an architecture. Every client shows the raw string and makes you parse it.
filters as four listsStatus, category, tag and tracker are four stacked lists. They're filters, never places: you can't see a category as a whole (its size, its disk, what's arriving there) or a tracker's health and your standing there.
details as a drawerTabs of fields: General, Files, Trackers, Peers. Thirty labelled values and no story. The pieces map, the most beautiful data a torrent has, is a thin strip.

3 · the thesis

Five questions, before any mechanism

People open a torrent client to answer a handful of questions. Each layout below starts from one of them. The engine's numbers are still there, one click or one key away, never gone.

When will it be ready?Time to ready as the loudest thing on a downloading row, and a runway of everything arriving.Today: the ETA column, 9th of 10.
Does anything need me?A short queue of what is broken or stuck, said in words, with the one action that fixes it. Empty most days, and saying so.Today: errored and stalled as rail counts.
What came in?What landed since you last looked, with "open" and "show in folder" right there, and a dot until you do.Today: "completed", sorted by nothing.
Where is my stuff?Categories and trackers as places with a size, a disk, a health and a story. The library as a library.Today: four filter lists.
Am I giving back?Ratio as a goal you're reaching, the rare ones you keep alive, what's safe to let go. The seed console already answers most of this.Today: the ratio column and board 22.
4 · try it

One window, six layouts

A working mock at base 14, 1180 × 740. "display" in the hero picks the layout, the grouping and what each row shows; click into the window and press v to cycle layouts. Click a torrent for its story. "+" opens the arrival sheet. The scope line (categories as chips, then trackers) replaces the four-list rail in every new layout; "classic" keeps today's rail and table.

L1

Rows that speak

answers when will it be ready · the table, grown up

The smallest step and probably the right default. Each torrent is two lines. The first is the clean name (parsed from the release name, the raw one a key away) with its chips, and on the right the one fact that matters for its state, big: ready in 7 min, 2.4×, waiting, landed 2 h ago. The second line is a bar that is the piece map in miniature (you see a sequential download, a scattered one, the gaps) and a sentence in plain words that would replace five columns.

forKeeps the list everyone knows; it's what Transmission did, a decade on. Works at every width (the sentence drops first, then the chips). Grouping by state, category, tracker or day added falls out for free with sticky headers that sum their group.
againstTwo lines per torrent: 14 rows in the window instead of 18. People with 3,000 torrents will want "compact" (one line, sentence hidden), which is close to classic again.
needsNothing new for the sentence (sync/maindata has it all). The piece bar wants torrents/pieceStates per visible row, polled slowly (every 5 s, only rows on screen), or a progress-only bar as a fallback. Clean names: a small MIT parser of our own, tested on real release names.
L2

Flow

answers does anything need me · what came in · when will it be ready

Not a list of torrents, a page about your downloads, read top to bottom like an arrivals board. Five lanes, each hiding when empty: needs you (broken or stuck, each with the action that fixes it; h snoozes one, as in Linear's inbox), arriving (a runway on a time axis: every bar ends at its ETA, queued and metadata bars are dashed), landed (the last day's completions, a dot until opened), giving back (ratio rings toward the goal, the rare ones marked), and resting (one line of totals). When nothing needs you, the top says so in one calm line.

forNobody else does this. It's the screen you want after a day away: what broke, what came, what's next. It turns "stalled" into a question with an answer ("no seed seen for 2 h · find another source / keep waiting").
againstIt's a summary, not a workbench: selecting 40 torrents to move them still wants rows. Lanes need rules (what counts as "needs you"; how long "landed" lasts) that must be predictable or people won't trust them.
needscompletion_on, eta, last_activity, seen_complete from maindata. "Not opened yet" is ours: the click on "open" in omaseed (or a notification click) clears it; we can't see a file manager open it. Lane rules become a DECISIONS entry.
L3

Shelves

answers where is my stuff

The library as a library: one shelf per category, one tile per torrent. The cover is drawn, not fetched: a two-letter monogram from the clean name in the category's colour, over the torrent's own piece grid, which fills in as it downloads. So every cover is unique, needs no network, and a download in progress is visibly half a cover. The year or version sits above, the state glyph in the corner.

forThe most "this isn't a torrent client" of the six. Lovely for media libraries and for people with tens or hundreds of torrents. Covers drawn from data keep Omarchy's look in every theme.
againstPoor at 3,000 torrents and at bulk work. Real posters (from TMDB or similar) would look richer but tell a third party what you download, need a key, and break the theme; if ever, an opt-in extension (board 34's extensions idea), never default.
needsCategory colours: a new per-category role pick stored in state.json (theme roles only, so it repaints). The cover grid reuses the piece map at a coarser size (16 × 6 cells).
L4

Map

answers what is using my disk · what can I let go

A treemap of the library by size, like DaisyDisk or Baobab, grouped by category or tracker. The colour is a choice: state (what's doing what), ratio (how much each has given back), last active (what nobody has asked for in months) or tracker. With "ratio" and "last active" it becomes the place to clean up: the big, dim tiles are the ones to let go, and the seeding coach (board 23) can point at them.

forOne picture answers a question the table can't. Pairs with delete (board 26's guards apply) and with the coach. Fun to watch while a big download fills its tile.
againstSmall torrents become slivers; names only fit in big tiles (hover and the status line carry the rest). A view you open now and then, not one you live in.
needsSizes and states from maindata. "Last active" from last_activity; "given since" from board 22's ledger. A squarified layout in C++ (a page of code) feeding a QML Repeater of rectangles.
L5

Places

answers where is my stuff · am I giving back, per category and tracker

Categories and trackers stop being filter lists and become cards you can read. A category card: how many, how big, a bar of its states, the disk it lives on and that disk's free space, what's arriving. A tracker card: whether it answers (a strip of the last 24 announces), what you've given there, your ratio, and for a private tracker the line that matters (ratio 0.94, your tracker asks for 1.0). Click a card to scope the window to it.

forMakes trackers visible, which matters most to private-tracker users (a big share of qBittorrent's users). Free space per save path, finally where you look for it. Doubles as the scope picker.
againstAnnounce history and "given per tracker" need recording over time, which only works while omaseed runs (it's resident, so mostly fine). Ratio rules per tracker are a new setting.
needstorrents/trackers per torrent (status, msg, next announce) sampled into a small ring buffer; per-tracker totals summed from maindata's tracker field; free space per path from sync's free_space_on_disk for the default path and our own statvfs for local paths (board 25 already does this).
L6

The story of a torrent

the details pane, rethought

Instead of four tabs of labelled fields, one scroll that tells you what's happening and how it got here. From the top: the clean name and one sentence of now; the piece map as the hero, big enough to read (pieces you have, pieces arriving this second, pieces only one peer has); the swarm as a constellation (you in the middle, seeds and peers around, a line for each active transfer, moving the way the bytes go) with where they are; the story as a timeline (added, files found, started, half, done, ratio goal, with the times between them); and files as bars by size. "All fields" at the bottom opens today's General tab, so nothing is lost.

1Now, in words. The sentence from the row, longer: who you're getting it from and what would make it faster. 2Pieces, big. 40 columns, one cell per piece group. White cells blink as they arrive. Yellow are rare. A click on the map toggles "first and last pieces first" (for previewing a video). 3The swarm. Green dots are seeds, blue are peers, dot size is speed. Lines move inward when you download from them and outward when you upload. The countries list comes from qBittorrent's own peer lookup when it's on. 4The story. Real events with real times: added (and from where: a magnet, a file, the browser), metadata, first byte, 50 %, done, ratio goal, moved, errors. Future steps dashed, with an estimate. 5Files by size. A bar each, skipped files struck through. The full tree (board 27's selection) one click away.
forThe pane finally says something on its own. The rare-piece warning and the story are new information, not new decoration. Works in the docked column and the bottom band (boards 12, 13) as one scroll.
againstA busy torrent's constellation with 80 peers needs a cap (the 24 fastest, "+56"). Power users will want the old tabs: keep them behind "all fields" or a toggle.
needstorrents/pieceStates and pieceHashes count; sync/torrentPeers (incremental, has country_code, client, speeds); rare pieces need availability per piece, which the API doesn't give: we'd approximate from peers' progress (good enough to flag "only one peer has the rest"). The story's events: added_on, completion_on, plus our ledger for the rest.
A1

The arrival sheet

adding a torrent, rethought

Today's add sheet is a form around a file tree. The arrival sheet leads with what's coming: the clean name and its chips; what's inside as one stacked bar by kind (video, subtitles, extras, text) with the junk already unticked; where it goes, with the category guessed from your own past choices and the disk bar showing after-the-add; and a guess at when it will be ready from the swarm and your usual speed. Board 25's checks (already in the library, files already on disk, free space) appear as notices in the same place. A magnet copied to the clipboard offers itself when the window gets focus, as Fragments does. And one new guard: a torrent that says it's a film but carries a program.

example
forAdding becomes a glance and one key. The program guard protects people from the most common torrent malware trick, and nobody does it. The category guess learns from what you already do; no rules to write.
againstA magnet shows nothing until its metadata comes (board 29's wait still applies; the sheet fills in as it arrives). A wrong guess must be easy to see and change, or it's worse than none.
needsParsing the .torrent locally (we do, board 25) or torrents/fetchMetadata for magnets (board 29). File kinds from extensions. The guess: count (resolution, kind, tracker) → category over your existing torrents; no network, no model.
12 · reaching the app

Opt-in, or the new default?

Not a "classic vs new" switch in settings. A global switch splits the app in two, doubles every test, and hides the new layouts from anyone who never opens settings. Linear, Notion and Finder solved this the same way: layout is a display option of the view, remembered per view, one key away. That's what "display" in §4 is.

Layouts are peers. Classic, rows, flow, shelves, map and places are six layouts of the same library, sharing the scope, the search, the selection, the palette and every action. A layout owns only how torrents are drawn. Classic stays forever (people who chose omaseed for today's table keep it).
Remembered per scope. "movies" can be shelves while "all" is rows and a private tracker is places. Saved in state.json beside the column widths. v cycles, V goes back; the palette has "layout: …" for each.
Suggested rollout. 1 · Clean names and the story pane (useful in classic too). 2 · Rows that speak, with grouping. 3 · Flow. 4 · The arrival sheet. 5 · Map and places. 6 · Shelves. Each step ships alone, behind nothing; each gets its own board first.
Default for new installs. Recommended: rows (L1) with grouping off, so a newcomer sees something better than a table without learning a new model; flow is one key away and mentioned once in the first-run tour. Existing users keep the layout they have (classic) and get one dismissible line about "display".
