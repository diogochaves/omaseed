The frame

Board 39 put new layouts into today's window and ran out of room. The top bar already holds eleven controls, the new layouts had nowhere for the servers but a different spot each, and the keys are painted on almost every surface. Before any layout gets its own board, this one settles the window around them: what sits on the left, at the top and at the bottom, where the servers live, and how the keys are taught. Six frames, each a different idea, in one live window.

§1 takes today's top bar and footer apart. §2 lists what any frame has to hold. §3 is the live window: switch the frame, the content, the width, one or two servers, a server going down, and how keys are shown. §4 explains each frame and where it comes from, §5 puts them side by side, §6 is the keys on their own, §7 is how board 39's layouts sit in the frame, §8 what the recommended path would replace, and §9 your calls. The theme switch repaints the mock.

2 · the brief

What any frame has to hold

The same in every layout.Board 39's layouts change the content area only. The rail, top and bottom stay put, so a layout switch never moves a control.
Servers in one place.At every width, in every layout. With two or more, a way to the others is always on screen; with one, nothing offers to switch (C12, D19).
A place to pick the scope.Board 39 recommends a layout remembered per category or tracker (its D4). That needs the categories and trackers somewhere you can see and pick.
A home for the notice.docs/35's band when a server stops answering or refuses the sign-in, with retry, edit login and switch server.
720 px.Half of a 1440 px screen when Hyprland tiles two windows. The frame has to keep its shape there, not shed half its buttons.
Keyboard-only and screen reader.Every control reachable without the mouse; a reading order that follows the regions; names without keys or glyphs (#78).
Omarchy.Theme roles, square corners, the monospace face. The bar widget already shows speeds and the server's state on every workspace (docs/20, C13).
Room to grow.Engines (board 36, C14) will add a server's engine and hidden features. A frame full on day one has no room for them.

6 · teaching the keys

Show keys where people look for them, not everywhere

omaseed stays keyboard-first. The question is only where the keys are written. Try each with "keys" in §3.

K1 · painted (today)Keys beside tabs, under the table in the footer, beside detail tabs. Learnable at a glance, but about twenty labels sit on screen at all times, most of them about the table even when it isn't in focus.
K2 · on demandKeys appear where you go looking: a tooltip ("retry now · R"), right-aligned in every menu as desktop menus do, beside every command in the palette, and the ? sheet. The footer carries status only and ends in one quiet "? keys". The palette becomes the teacher: run a command once from it and you've seen its key.
K3 · on demand, plus alt (recommended)K2, and while alt is held every control shows its key as a small badge, as Vimium's link hints and Office's key tips do. Nothing is painted until you ask, and the whole map is one key away. Alt has no binding in Keymap today, and Omarchy's own bindings use super.
K4 · painted, then fadingK1 for the first two weeks (or the first 100 launches), then K2. Kind to newcomers, but the window changes under you one day, and a returning user has to find the setting to get the hints back.
  • Screen readers are not affected by any of them: since #78 a control's name never contains its key; the key is its description.
  • Board 39 used v for "display". v is already the range select. The mocks here give display no key; it is in the menu and the palette.
  • The detail tabs' 1–4 stay keys under every option; only their labels go.
7 · board 39 in the frame

The frame holds still; the content changes

  • One frame for every layout. Classic, rows, flow, shelves, map and places all sit in the same content area. Nothing around them moves when you switch.
  • The rail is the scope. Board 39 replaced the rail with a line of chips in its new layouts (its D7). With frame A the rail stays in every layout, so that line goes, and so does its second home for the servers.
  • Display sits at the head of the content, beside the scope's name, because it changes the content and nothing else. It is remembered per scope, as board 39's D4 recommends ("movies" as shelves, "all" as rows).
  • At 720 px the rail becomes icons with the server dots at its foot, so the servers are still bottom left.
